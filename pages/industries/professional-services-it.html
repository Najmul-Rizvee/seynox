Where it goes wrong

The four failures we see in this sector.

Not hypotheticals. These are the findings that come up again and again on first documentation.

Everyone works from somewhere else

Client site, home, airport. Access rules written for one office quietly block half the firm, so people route around them with personal accounts.

Client data arrives in every format

Files come by email, by link, by USB stick. Without a defined landing place, client material spreads to personal drives within a week.

Utilisation is the metric

An hour of downtime is an hour nobody bills. The business case for monitoring is arithmetic, not fear.

Growth by hiring in bursts

Three people start the same Monday. Onboarding needs to be a documented ten-minute sequence, not an afternoon of guessing which licences to buy.

What we put in place

Six things, in order
01
Identity that assumes mobility

Conditional access built for people who travel, with MFA that does not punish them for it and device compliance that keeps client data off unmanaged laptops.

02
A defined place for client material

Structured by client and engagement, with external sharing controls and links that expire — so a handover is not an archaeology project.

03
Onboarding and offboarding runbooks

A documented sequence with the licence, the group, the folders, and the equipment. Same result whoever runs it.

04
Backups and retention that fit engagements

Retention matched to how long you contractually keep client material, and restores tested monthly rather than assumed.

05
Cost control on licences

Quarterly licence review. Firms hiring in bursts almost always carry seats for people who left and tiers nobody uses.

06
Documentation the firm owns

Register, diagrams, tenant ownership. Useful in day-to-day work, and a due-diligence asset if you ever sell or merge.

Professional services questions

We are fully remote. Do we still need documentation?+

More, not less. With no office to walk into, the register and the diagram are the only reliable description of how your firm runs.

Can you support people travelling internationally?+

Yes, with conditional access rules that allow travel without leaving the door open. We will show you the trade-offs rather than decide for you.

Do you handle device purchasing?+

We specify and configure; you buy direct. We take no hardware margin, which keeps the advice honest.

How fast is onboarding a new starter?+

Once the runbook exists, about ten minutes of engineer time. The first one takes a couple of hours because we are writing the runbook.
